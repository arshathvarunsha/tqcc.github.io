About TQCC

TQCC (Theoretical, Quantum, and Computational Chemistry Research Talks) is a student-driven seminar series focused on modern theory and computation in chemistry and materials science. Our discussions span a wide range of topics, including polaritons and cavity quantum electrodynamics, light–matter interactions, non-adiabatic dynamics, and excited-state processes. We also highlight emerging directions such as machine learning (ML) and AI for chemistry, data-driven modeling, and advanced computational techniques (e.g., electronic structure methods, molecular dynamics ..).

TQCC aims to make cutting-edge research easy to follow, especially for graduate students from different backgrounds. Speakers are encouraged to start with the main ideas, explain important terms, and build intuition before going into details. This format helps the audience learn the fundamentals while still engaging with current research challenges.

Beyond learning, TQCC is a space to exchange ideas, get feedback, and connect across subfields. We aim to foster new collaborations by bringing together researchers who work on complementary methods and problems. Whether you are exploring a new topic, looking for technical input, or searching for potential collaborators, TQCC is designed to be a welcoming place for discussion.

Speaker: Dr. Mohsen Yarmohammadi — Research Scientist, Georgetown University

Title: Optical Control of Quantum Materials

Abstract: Quantum materials exhibit emergent phenomena, such as unconventional superconductivity, topological order, and complex magnetism, that arise from strong interactions, reduced dimensionality, and symmetry constraints. While traditional control parameters such as temperature, pressure, or magnetic fields probe equilibrium phases, tailored ultrafast optical fields provide access to entirely new regions of the energy landscape. In this talk, Dr. Yarmohammadi will discuss how coherent light can be used to dynamically engineer quantum materials far from equilibrium.
